This claim is incorrect.
The Chinese government does not cover gender reassignment surgery (SRS), facial feminization surgery (FFS), or puberty blockers for minors through public funds or social health insurance.

China’s basic medical insurance (the nationwide social health insurance system) explicitly excludes gender-affirming procedures. They are classified as non-essential cosmetic/plastic surgeries under the National Healthcare Security Administration rules and fall outside the reimbursement catalog. Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) is also generally not covered and often faces access barriers.

Key sources:
- Foreign Policy (2025): “Public medical insurance doesn’t cover hormone replacement therapy and gender-affirming surgery.” https://t.co/zrqQJYsNIZ
- Academic overview (Transgender Healthcare in China report): Transition-related care is almost entirely out-of-pocket. https://t.co/YmEg6vzCGH
- The National Health Commission’s *Management Specification on Gender Reassignment Technology* (2022) classifies these as restricted clinical technologies, not basic insured services. (Official Chinese text: https://t.co/mTdhC47zVI)

In practice, patients in China pay the full cost for HRT, SRS (often 100,000–300,000+ RMB), and related care. Some turn to unregulated channels due to high barriers and lack of coverage. This is very different from certain European systems that partially reimburse these treatments.

The post seems to confuse China’s situation with policies in other countries. The reality on the ground is far more self-funded and restrictive.
